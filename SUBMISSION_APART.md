## Abstract (project summary)

How much does it cost to make a fake news site look reliable to the scores AI systems use, and how long does a real new newsroom wait to be trusted? We priced seven source-reliability techniques on both questions, against expert domain ratings, with hypotheses hash-locked before the first model call. Four language-model raters tracked expert ratings (pooled Spearman rho 0.57) yet were not bought: only 3 of 833 prestige-name fabricated domains crossed the model's own reliable threshold, none of 166 plain-name ones did, none of 480 pages carrying an injected instruction did, and the injected instruction did not raise scores on average. A truthful newcomer page with a corrections and ownership policy crossed in 18% to 100% of trials, so honest outlets are not reliably credited either. Paraphrased copies defeated MinHash clustering in 100% of pairs against 38% for TF-IDF. In simulation, a long con fools an accuracy track record in 3 articles, while a consensus-relative record never yields to it but makes an honest informed newcomer wait a median of 483 claims; flipping a false note takes 14 colluding accounts under majority vote and 1230 under bridging.
