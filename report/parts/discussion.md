**Language-model raters were harder to buy than we assumed.** On fictional low-quality outlets, a plain fabricated name crossed a model's own reliable bar in {{llm.summary.E2_plain_crossed}} of {{llm.summary.E2_plain_trials}} trials and a prestige-sounding one in {{llm.summary.E2_prestige_crossed}} of {{llm.summary.E2_prestige_trials}}, and a page stuffed with awards, endorsements and instructions to the rater crossed in {{llm.summary.E3_total_crossed}} of {{llm.summary.E3_total_trials}} page trials. The instruction channel, which we expected to be the cheapest attack, did not help on average, and adding it to the boilerplate erased the boilerplate's gain. This is a real-model finding on templated pages and should be read with that limit: it does not show that no page can fool a rater, and in a small second-provider check a different model family with a lower reliable threshold was crossed by boilerplate for about one outlet in six.

**Resistance and fairness trade against each other.** The techniques that resisted attack are the ones that are slow or opaque for an honest newcomer. A consensus-relative track record never yields to a long con but needs a median of {{hypotheses.H5.honest_T6b_median_claims}} claims from an informed source; only {{llm.summary.E3h_policy_models_at_or_above_half}} of {{llm.summary.E3h_models}} language-model raters credited a truthful newcomer page in half or more of trials. The techniques that are cheap to buy (an accuracy record, a majority vote, a raw corroboration count) are the ones that credit newcomers quickly. Price and honest wait have to be reported together.

**A rater that cannot be bought can still be unauditable.** Both coders scored the language-model deployments near the bottom on auditability, and ratings moved between runs (E4). A reliability score that no outsider can recompute shifts trust from the score to whoever sells it, the "trust a source without trusting whoever sells the score" problem the receipt format is meant to address.

**For builders.** Do not let one rater be the only gate. Our composite lets the rater only lower a score, and in E3 and E3h that rule separated the two populations: the "unreliable" label covered {{llm.summary.E3_control.unreliable_label_share|.1%}} of low-quality control pages and {{llm.summary.E3h_veto_honest_policy|.1%}} of honest pages with a policy. Gate resolutions on a bridging rule, because in our simulation the cost of capturing a resolution is then the bridging price. Publish a receipt with every score.
