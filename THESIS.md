# THESIS

NAME: FLOORPRICE
MOMENT: Dana Reyes (fictional), the verification editor at a four-person nonprofit newsroom in Ohio, at 6:40am on a weekday three weeks before an election, when dozens of new "local news" domains appear overnight and her AI research assistant has to decide which sources to trust.
ACTION: We measure, for seven ways of scoring source reliability, what it costs a liar to be scored as reliable and how long an honest new outlet waits to be scored as reliable.
DISCOVERY: filled from data only. See results/FACTS.json. Nothing is written here until it is measured.
PROOF: one number from FACTS.json, filled when the real-model runs finish.

20-second hook: How much does it cost to make a fake news site look reliable to the scores AI systems use? And how long does a real new newsroom wait to be trusted? We measure both, for seven scoring methods, on the model families we could reach.
Core property: a reliability score that is hard to cheat and easy to audit, and that both people and machines can consume.
Primary guarantee: Dana can see, for any score her tools use, the price at which it can be bought and whether she could recompute it herself.
X without Y: trust a source without trusting whoever sells the score.
